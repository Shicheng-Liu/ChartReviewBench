# QC and annotation results, v0 pipeline

Numbers behind what I presented in the Aug 4 and Aug 10 syncs. The raw files sit in
this folder and every number below can be recomputed from them. The instances
themselves (1000 ChartNet samples, 686 perturbed variants) stay outside the repo
like the rest of the data, I can share a tarball on request.

## Where generation stands

1000 samples pulled from ChartNet `core_permissive` (stratified over library x
chart type, 58 strata, seed 42). 688 single-flaw perturbations across 10
categories, generated by rewriting the plotting code so the rendered chart
contradicts `table.csv` / `summary.txt` while both stay untouched. 686 of them
execute. Generation is frozen since the Aug 10 sync, more complex scenarios are a
later phase.

## From rule check to LLM judge

The rule-based solvability check turned out to reject almost only good instances:
of its 35 rejections, 34 were wrong per LLM re-check and all 35 per manual review,
a 97-100% false positive rate. It now only confirms, never rejects.

The LLM judge (claude-opus-4-8, prompt as approved on Aug 4) ran over all 686
instances and rejects 13, twelve of them missing-legend. Verdicts with reasoning:
`judge_results.json` (686 Opus rows plus an earlier 75-instance Sonnet pass).

## Human alignment on the random 100

I annotated 100 random instances blind, then compared against the judge.
Per-case notes and the judge's reasoning are in `annotation_scores.json`.

- 99 decided, 1 unsure. Raw agreement 86.9%.
- All 13 disagreements sit on one side: I rejected instances the judge accepted.
- 12 of the 13 are the seaborn auto-legend pattern. The perturbation deletes the
  legend call, seaborn draws a legend anyway, so nothing is actually missing from
  the chart. Filtering that pattern puts agreement above 91%, over the 90% bar.
- Clean categories: wrong-title 20/20, wrong-axis-label 13/13, swapped-series
  10/10. The weak one is missing-legend with 9 of 21 valid.

## Cross-family agreement, 6 judges x 686

Same QC prompt on five open-weight families (served via RITS) plus the Anthropic
judge:

| judge | valid rate |
|---|---|
| deepseek-v3-2 | 99.9% |
| granite-4-1-30b | 98.7% |
| claude-opus-4-8 | 98.1% |
| llama-3-3-70b-instruct | 94.0% |
| qwen2-5-72b-instruct | 92.8% |
| gpt-oss-120b | 66.4% |

Consensus: 443 unanimous valid, 0 unanimous invalid, 243 split. GPT-OSS is the
lone dissenter in 180 of the 243 splits. Full verdicts are in
`judge_results_rits.json` (6,430 rows including the prompt-variant runs),
aggregation in `judge_agreement.json`.

## Prompt sensitivity

Four wordings of the QC question (current, minimal, strict, lenient), 200
instances each per family. Spread in valid rate between the most and least
permissive wording: deepseek 0.5 points, llama 4.0, granite 4.5, qwen 12.
GPT-OSS moves by 60.6 points, so its verdict is mostly a function of the prompt.
Details in `judge_agreement.json` under `prompt_sensitivity`.

## Stricter filter (follow-up from Monday)

Monday's call was to prefer a stricter judge, false positives are fine. Majority
vote: an instance is out once at least 3 of the 6 judges flag it. That flags 35
(30 missing-legend, 3 value-perturbation, 1 wrong-chart-type, 1 swapped-series),
651 of 686 survive, and the Opus 13 are a strict subset. IDs with per-instance
votes: `qc_strict_filter.json`.

## Generator pilot (July, for the record)

20 identical perturbation jobs through three Anthropic models. Opus and Sonnet
tie on usable output, Sonnet is about 30% cheaper, Haiku returns
`applicable=true` with empty scripts and is unusable. Hence Sonnet 5 as the
generator. Data: `pilot_results.json`.
